Hidden drought: where rain looks normal but groundwater is running low

Map Methods

How this works

India's drought declarations are triggered mainly by rainfall (Manual for Drought Management 2020). Groundwater is only an optional impact indicator. So a district with normal rain but falling groundwater can't be declared ("hidden drought"), and a district with poor rain but healthy groundwater may be declared while it is actually buffered. This site puts both signals side by side for every district.

This site is not an official drought declaration. It is an independent view built from public government data.

A district gets no category when either side is unknown: too few wells, no rain data, or a rain season that started less than 15 days ago (for example the October–December monsoon districts in early October).

Rain: when is it "short"?

Following the Drought Manual 2020, rain counts as short if any one of these holds for the district's current or most recent season:

TestRule
Rainfall deviationIMD category Deficient or worse (20% or more below normal)
SPIStandardised Precipitation Index for the season of −1.0 or lower ("moderately dry")
Dry spell4 or more consecutive weeks, each with less than half the normal rain for that week (the manual: "usually 4 weeks"). Weeks only count once the season's rain is normally under way, so dry weeks before the monsoon's usual arrival don't count.

The dry-spell test catches badly timed seasons. Gujarat in 2023 had above-normal rain overall but almost no rain for five weeks in August, so its districts count as rain short. The district panel always says which test applied.

Seasons. June–September (south-west monsoon) for most districts. For the 99 districts IMD reports under the October–December monsoon (all of Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Andhra Pradesh and Puducherry, and South Interior Karnataka), the season switches on 1 October. Outside a season, the last completed season is shown.

Which number. The headline departure is IMD's official district figure where IMD publishes one. Otherwise (about 70 districts) it is IMD's 0.25° gridded rainfall averaged over the district area, labelled "gridded". Normals are 1971–2020, the same period IMD uses. At the level of IMD's 36 subdivisions, our gridded figures match IMD's to within 2 percentage points (median).

Groundwater: when is it "low"?

Wells are measured by the Central Ground Water Board (CGWB) four times a year (January, pre-monsoon April–May, August, November), and many newer wells report every six hours. Each well is compared only with its own past readings at the same time of year:

  • Percentile: the share of past years in which water was shallower than now. 10 means water is deeper now than in 90% of past years. 20 or less counts as low.
  • The district value is the median across its wells.
ConfidenceRule
Full5 or more wells, each with 10 or more years of history for that time of year
Short record3 or more wells with 5 or more years
Provisionalat least 1 well with 2–4 years. Shown in a lighter shade. With so few years, "deepest on record" is easy to reach, so treat provisional results as an early signal.
Level onlywells are reporting but have no history yet: depth and change are shown, but no category

Most automatic wells started in 2023, so many districts are provisional today. Each year adds one more year of history. Where a new automatic well sits at the same site as an older manual well and their readings agree (within 1.5 m in at least two shared seasons), the old manual record becomes its history. Wells are assigned to districts by their location, not by the district name in the source file. Physically impossible values (shallower than −5 m or deeper than 150 m) are removed. Big year-to-year changes are normal and are kept.

Long-term trend is the median rate of change in each well's pre-monsoon depth from 2000 to 2023 (Sen's slope), for wells with 10 or more years.

Groundwater stress

From IN-GRES (CGWB and IIT Hyderabad), Dynamic Ground Water Resources 2025-26, used exactly as published: stage of extraction = annual extraction ÷ extractable resource. Categories: Safe, Semi-critical, Critical, Over-exploited, and Saline (assessed separately). District totals can hide stressed blocks, so each district also shows its worst block, and flags districts where any block is Critical or Over-exploited.

Drought index

The Combined Drought Index from the India Drought Monitor (Water and Climate Lab, IIT Gandhinagar), weekly since July 2021, averaged over each district's area. Classes D0 (abnormally dry) to D4 (exceptional) use IDM's own thresholds. This is a detected drought measure, not an official declaration.

Looking back

Use the Date menu to see the categories at the end of January, May (mid-summer), August and November of any year since 2000, the times groundwater is measured. Some dates have few or no categories because the source data is missing: May 2020 and May 2021 (no pre-monsoon survey during COVID), August 2012 and January 2016 (survey rounds largely absent from the public data), and 2024 onward (newer wells, short history). For past years, rain is our gridded figure. Official IMD figures are kept from October 2026 onward.

Limits

  • Groundwater is local. A district median can hide very different aquifers and wells.
  • The district map is the December 2023 version. Rajasthan's nine districts abolished in December 2024 are still shown, and districts created since then (e.g. Pandhurna, Maihar, Mauganj, Vav-Tharad) are not.
  • Four island districts (Andaman & Nicobar, Lakshadweep) are outside IMD's gridded rainfall; where IMD lists them, the official figure is used.
  • The Drought Manual's full decision also uses crop, vegetation and soil-moisture evidence and field checks, which this site does not replicate.
